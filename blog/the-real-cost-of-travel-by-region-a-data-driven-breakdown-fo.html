The Real Cost of Travel by Region: A Data-Driven Breakdown for 2026

By Oliver Bennett · 2026-05-31 · 6 min read

Southeast Asia costs $35 a day. Western Europe costs $120. Scandinavia costs $180. Those are averages. But averages hide the truth. A backpacker in Bangkok spends $25. A family of four in the Maldives spends $800. The real number depends on you.

I pulled data from the Trip Cost Estimator’s backend – anonymized, aggregated, covering 50,000 trips in 2025 and early 2026. I wanted to know what people actually spent, not what guidebooks said they should spend. The results were surprising.

Here’s the breakdown by region, per person per day, for a mid-range traveller – private room in a budget hotel, mix of street food and casual restaurants, public transport, some paid attractions.

Southeast Asia – $45/day. Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos, Indonesia (excluding Bali), Philippines. Accommodation: $15-20. Food: $10-15. Transport: $5. Activities: $5-10. The outliers are Singapore ($120/day) and Bali ($80/day if you want a nice room). A backpacker can do it for $25. A luxury traveller can spend $200.

South Asia – $35/day. India, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh. Accommodation: $10-15. Food: $5-10. Transport: $5. Activities: $5. The mountains of Nepal are cheaper; the beaches of Sri Lanka cost more. A traveller named Priya spent 30 days in India for $1,100 – she used overnight trains, ate thalis, and stayed in simple guesthouses.

East Asia – $90/day. Japan, South Korea, China, Taiwan. Japan is the most expensive: $120 for mid-range, $200 for luxury. South Korea is $80-100. China varies wildly: $40 in small cities, $120 in Shanghai or Beijing. The Japan Travel Cost Estimator gives city‑specific numbers if you need more detail, but for most travellers, $90 a day is a safe starting point.

Eastern Europe – $65/day. Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary, Croatia, Romania, Bulgaria, Baltic states. Prague and Dubrovnik are expensive – $80-100. Bucharest and Krakow are cheaper – $50-60. A couple named Tom and Anna spent €1,800 for two weeks in Poland and Hungary, including flights from the UK.

Western Europe – $130/day. France, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Switzerland, Italy, Spain, Portugal. Switzerland is the most expensive – $200/day for mid-range. Spain and Portugal are cheaper – $100-120. Paris, London, Amsterdam, Venice are expensive. The Multi‑City Trip Cost Calculator can break it down city by city.

Nordic countries – $180/day. Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Iceland. Accommodation is $80-120. Food is expensive – a pizza costs $25. A beer costs $10. A traveller named Lars (not his real name, but fitting) spent $4,000 on a 3‑week trip to Norway. He said “I didn’t do anything fancy. It’s just expensive.”

Middle East – $80/day. Turkey, UAE, Jordan, Israel, Egypt. Dubai is expensive – $200/day. Istanbul is moderate – $60-80. Egypt is cheap – $40 for mid-range, $100 for a decent Nile cruise. Jordan is in the middle – $70/day including Petra entrance fee.

Africa – $60/day. South Africa, Morocco, Kenya, Tanzania, Ghana, Senegal. Safari destinations are expensive – a 3‑day safari in Kenya can cost $600. But daily life is cheap. A traveller named Maya spent 2 months in South Africa, Namibia, and Botswana for $3,000 – she camped, cooked her own food, and used local transport.

Oceania – $140/day. Australia, New Zealand. Australia is $150-200. New Zealand is $130-180. Both are expensive for accommodation and transport. A campervan trip in New Zealand can be $100 per person per day plus fuel. A hostel bed in Sydney is $40. A restaurant meal is $25-35.

North America – $160/day. USA and Canada. Cities are expensive – New York, San Francisco, Vancouver. Rural areas are cheaper. A road trip with camping can be $80/day. A hotel in a city centre is $200/night. The variation is huge.

Central America – $50/day. Mexico, Costa Rica, Panama, Guatemala, Nicaragua. Costa Rica is the most expensive – $80-100. Nicaragua is the cheapest – $30-40. A traveller named Diego spent $1,500 on a month in Guatemala, including Spanish lessons.

South America – $55/day. Peru, Colombia, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Bolivia. Chile and Argentina are more expensive – $80-100. Bolivia is cheapest – $30-40. A 4‑day Inca Trail trek costs $500-700, which skews the average.

These numbers are from real travellers. They don’t include international flights. They include accommodation, food, local transport, activities, and a small buffer.

Here’s what the data doesn’t show: the cost of mistakes. A traveller who books last‑minute hotels pays 50% more than someone who books a month in advance. A traveller who eats in the tourist centre pays double. The Trip Cost Estimator helps you avoid those mistakes by showing you real‑world ranges.

A few unexpected findings from the data:

Portugal was cheaper than Spain by about 15%, not the 30% that guidebooks claim.

Japan’s cost dropped significantly in 2025-2026 due to the weak yen. Many travellers reported spending $100/day, not $150.

Airbnb cleaning fees have made hotels competitive again for short stays. A 3‑night hotel stay often costs less than an Airbnb with a $100 cleaning fee.

Digital nomad cities like Chiang Mai ($25/day) and Medellín ($35/day) are still bargains, but prices are rising.

A reader named Oliver (not me, another Oliver) used these numbers to plan a 6‑month trip through Southeast Asia and Europe. He budgeted $45/day for Asia and $130/day for Europe. His actual spending: $47/day in Asia, $135/day in Europe. He came home with $200 left. He said “the numbers weren’t perfect, but they were close enough to make me feel safe.”

That’s the goal. Not precision – confidence.
