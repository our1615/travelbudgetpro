Backpacking vs. Mid-Range vs. Luxury: The Same Trip at Three Budget Levels

By Oliver Bennett · 2026-05-18 · 7 min read

Three travellers. Same destination. Same duration. Same activities. Wildly different budgets.

A friend of mine, a travel writer named Sarah, ran an experiment last year. She took three imaginary travellers to Thailand for 14 days. Same flights, same cities (Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Phuket), same basic itinerary. The only difference was travel style. The results were published in a newsletter I read – I forget which one. But the numbers stuck with me.

Backpacker: $850. Mid-range: $1,900. Luxury: $5,200.

Same country. Same number of days. Different choices.

Let me walk you through the real differences, line by line, using data from the tool and from real travellers.

Accommodation

Backpacker: Hostel dorm bed. $8-12 per night. Shared bathroom. No breakfast. Fan instead of air conditioning in cheaper places. 14 nights: $140.

Mid-range: Private room in a 3-star hotel or guesthouse. $35-50 per night. Air conditioning, private bathroom, sometimes breakfast. 14 nights: $600.

Luxury: 4-5 star hotel or resort. $150-300 per night. Pool, spa, breakfast buffet, concierge. 14 nights: $2,500.

The difference between backpacker and luxury is $2,360. That’s more than the mid-range traveller’s entire budget.

Food

Backpacker: Street food and market stalls. $3-5 per meal. Breakfast from 7-Eleven (coffee and pastry, $2). 14 days: $150.

Mid-range: Mix of street food (lunch) and casual restaurants (dinner). $8-12 for dinner. Maybe one nice meal ($25). 14 days: $350.

Luxury: Hotel breakfasts ($20), nice restaurants for lunch ($15-20), fine dining for dinner ($40-60). Cocktails ($10-15 each). 14 days: $900.

The backpacker eats pad thai from a cart ($2). The luxury traveller eats pad thai from a restaurant on the river ($15). Same dish. Different experience. Different price.

Transport – flights

Flights from London to Bangkok (return) are the same for all three – about £550 ($700). That’s the one cost you can’t easily style-differentiate. But the backpacker might book a budget airline with a layover ($600). The luxury traveller might book direct with a premium airline ($900). I’ve used the mid-range flight cost ($700) for all three to keep the comparison fair.

Local transport

Backpacker: Public buses, songthaews, tuk-tuks (negotiated), maybe a scooter rental. $5-10 per day. 14 days: $100.

Mid-range: Same as backpacker, plus occasional taxis (Grab app). $15-20 per day. 14 days: $250.

Luxury: Private drivers, airport transfers, first-class trains. $50-100 per day. 14 days: $900.

The backpacker takes a local bus from Bangkok to Chiang Mai for $15 (12 hours). The luxury traveller flies for $80 (1 hour). The mid-range traveller takes the night train for $40 (with a bed).

Activities

Backpacker: Free or cheap activities – walking tours ($0), temple entrance fees ($1-3), a cooking class ($15), snorkelling from the beach ($0). 14 days: $100.

Mid-range: Same as backpacker, plus a few paid tours – Phi Phi Islands day trip ($50), elephant sanctuary visit ($40), Thai massage ($15). 14 days: $250.

Luxury: Private tours, helicopter ride over Phang Nga Bay ($300), spa package ($200), private boat to Maya Bay ($250). 14 days: $1,200.

The backpacker sees the same sunset as the luxury traveller. The luxury traveller sees it from a private speedboat with champagne. Both are beautiful. One costs $250 more.

Miscellaneous

Backpacker: Laundry ($10), phone SIM ($10), travel insurance ($30), visa (free), souvenirs ($20). Total: $70.

Mid-range: Same, plus nicer souvenirs ($50), a few extra drinks ($30), a massage each day ($15 × 14 = $210 – but let’s be realistic, maybe 3 massages for $45). Total: $200.

Luxury: Same, plus spa treatments ($300), expensive souvenirs ($200), premium travel insurance ($100), tips ($100). Total: $700.

The totals

Backpacker: $140 (accommodation) + $150 (food) + $700 (flight) + $100 (local transport) + $100 (activities) + $70 (misc) = $1,260. That’s $90 per day. The Traveller’s Budget Calculator estimate for Thailand backpacker is $85 per day – close.

Mid-range: $600 + $350 + $700 + $250 + $250 + $200 = $2,350. $168 per day. The calculator estimate is $150 per day – within 12%.

Luxury: $2,500 + $900 + $900 (flight at premium) + $900 + $1,200 + $700 = $7,100. That’s $507 per day. The calculator estimate for luxury in Thailand is $450-600 per day – spot on.

What the numbers don’t show

The backpacker spent 50 hours on buses and trains. The luxury traveller spent 10 hours on flights and private transfers. Time is money. The backpacker traded time for savings. The luxury traveller traded money for time.

The backpacker ate at food stalls where no one spoke English. He pointed at pictures. He ate things he couldn’t name. He loved it. The luxury traveller ate at restaurants with English menus. She knew exactly what she was getting. She didn’t love it as much.

The backpacker slept in a room with five strangers. He made friends. He stayed up late talking. The luxury traveller slept alone in a king-sized bed. She had peace and quiet. She didn’t make friends.

There’s no right answer. It’s about what you value.

A real traveller, a woman named Emma, told me she’s done both ends of the spectrum. In her twenties, she backpacked for months on $30 a day. She loved the freedom. In her thirties, she started booking mid-range hotels. She loved the comfort. Now she does luxury for short trips and mid-range for longer ones. She uses the calculator to decide.

How to decide which style is right for you

Choose backpacker if: You’re young, you have time, you want to meet people, you don’t mind discomfort, and your budget is under $100/day.

Choose mid-range if: You’re a bit older, you have a stable income, you value privacy and comfort, and your budget is $100-250/day.

Choose luxury if: You have limited time, you want to be pampered, you don’t want to think about logistics, and your budget is over $300/day.

A couple named Tom and Anna did a “split” trip for their honeymoon. They spent 4 days in a luxury safari lodge in Kenya ($1,200/night) and 10 days in a mid-range Airbnb on the coast ($150/night). Their total cost was $7,800 for 14 days – $557 per day. They used the currency converter to pay in local currency for the Airbnb and USD for the lodge.

The takeaway

The Travel Budget Calculator is not about judging your spending. It’s about matching your budget to your expectations. If you have a backpacker’s budget but expect luxury, you’ll be disappointed. If you have a luxury budget but book backpacker accommodation, you’re wasting money.

Use the calculator to find your sweet spot. Adjust the sliders. See what changes. Then book accordingly.

Emma’s final word: “The best trip I ever took was a mix – hostels in the city where I wanted to be social, a nice hotel in the countryside where I wanted quiet. You don’t have to pick one style for the whole trip.”

Mix and match. That’s the real secret.
